Known failure modes and limitations

  • T waves and tall P waves. A single-lead Pan-Tompkins pass can mistake large T waves for R peaks. The ensemble vote gate suppresses them but can also erase genuine low-amplitude beats.
  • Extreme noise. High hum, wander and EMG amplitudes saturate the adaptive threshold. Precision falls before sensitivity does, visible in the noisy stress preset.
  • Vote sensitivity. Requiring too many leads drops sensitivity sharply; requiring too few floods the output with false positives. The sweep below shows this trade-off on the current record.
  • Short records. HRV frequency estimates need long, stationary R-R series. Records under a couple of minutes produce unstable LF/HF ratios and should not be interpreted physiologically.
  • Synthetic ground truth. The reference R peaks come from the generator's own phase crossings. Scores measure agreement with the model, not clinical accuracy on real recordings.
  • Model mismatch. The McSharry generator is a smooth ODE, not real electrophysiology. Arrhythmias, ectopic beats, electrode detachment and pacemaker spikes are not modelled.
  • Not a medical device. Synthetic signals and detector outputs are for education and research only. Never use them for diagnosis, treatment, monitoring or clinical decisions.

Documentation

How the engine works, what each module does, and how to run the verification suite.

Engine modules

src/cardio.js
McSharry ODE 12-lead synthesis, lead projection matrix, seeded RR series, additive hum, wander and EMG artefacts, and physiological scaling.
src/filters.js
Analogue Butterworth prototype, low-pass to band-pass and band-stop transforms, bilinear transform, SOS pairing, causal filtering, zero-phase forward-backward filtering and magnitude response.
src/analysis.js
Notch and high-pass conditioning, Pan-Tompkins QRS detection, multi-lead voting ensemble, detection scoring, HRV time and frequency metrics, quadratic spline interpolation and Welch PSD.

Signal pipeline

  1. Seeded RR intervals combine mean rate, Mayer wave, respiratory sinus arrhythmia and Gaussian jitter.
  2. A fourth-order Runge-Kutta integrator advances 12 coupled oscillators at 4x the output sampling rate.
  3. Each lead is projected through the lead matrix, rescaled to millivolts, and given an independent noise realisation.
  4. The detector band-passes 5 to 15 Hz, differentiates, squares, integrates over 120 ms, then thresholds with adaptive signal and noise estimates.
  5. The ensemble clusters detections across leads, gates weak clusters, and refines onto the spatial magnitude maximum.
  6. HRV metrics are computed from detected R-R intervals with a 100 Hz interpolated Welch spectrum.

Run and test

Open index.html directly in any modern browser. No build step, server, package install or network access is required. The three source modules are UMD wrappers, so they load as plain scripts in the browser and as CommonJS modules under Node.

npm test
node --test test/*.test.js

The suite covers filter design, SOS stability, zero-phase symmetry, generator determinism, ground-truth scoring, HRV invariants, spline and PSD maths, DOM structure, accessibility wiring and mobile layout constraints.

Keyboard and accessibility

  • Tabs follow the WAI-ARIA tab pattern: Left/Right or Home/End to move, Enter or Space to activate.
  • Number keys 1 through 6 jump to a tab. R regenerates the record.
  • Every control has a visible label and a live output. Canvases carry text alternatives and the numeric results are also published as tables.
  • Plots use a colour-blind safe palette and never rely on colour alone: markers differ by shape and line style.
